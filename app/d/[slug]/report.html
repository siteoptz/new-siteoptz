Existing patients also come through paid media — tracked as a separate lane

Of the 1,698 paid leads matched to a patient record, 477 were new (the ROAS above) and 1,221 were existing patients who reached the practice through a paid lead. They generate real revenue, but it is valued separately, not blended into new-patient ROAS: existing-patient leads are first filtered to ad-responsive visits (a new appointment or service line — not routine reschedule, billing, or records calls) and then valued at finance's per-encounter revenue ($809 per patient per year), pending isolation. Reported as a distinct lead-forward lane so the headline acquisition number stays clean and no revenue is double-counted.

What the Board Should Take Away

$ Media is profitable and converts well

477 new patients × $1,200 = $572,400 in new-patient LTV on $35,576 of measured spend — a 16.1× return, the strongest of the three practices.

! $1.37M at risk if media stops

477 new patients over five months = $572,400 in new-patient lifetime value; annualized at the current pace, ~$1.37M per year that paid media produced and would forfeit if turned off.

↑ The lever is scale, not capture

At a measured 16.1× return, 36% match, and 10.2% conversion, the funnel is efficient and cheap. Additional qualified spend should compound — the opportunity is volume.

✓ Why the return is this high

This practice runs the lowest cost-per-lead in the portfolio (~$7.61) on high-volume branded and local search, and the same spend also drives online bookings counted in other channels — so 16.1× is a conservative, phone-matched floor.

Every metric is matched

Each paid-lead phone was traced to EMR encounters (lead-forward); new patients are valued at $1,200 lifetime value (~2 visits at ~$809 average annual revenue per patient). Paid media produced 477 new patients = $572,400 in new-patient LTV on $35,576 of measured spend (16.1×); existing-patient paid-driven visits are tracked as a separate lane. Spend is measured Jan–May 2026.

Most conversion rate optimization is sold as a practice built for e-commerce traffic volumes - hundreds of thousands of monthly visitors, tests reaching significance in days. A multi-location healthcare group or a self-storage portfolio rarely has that traffic at the page level. Testing a landing page that gets four hundred visits a month will not reach statistical significance before the underlying campaign, season, or price changes anyway, and running the test regardless just produces a result that looks decisive and is not.

The larger problem is where the losses actually happen. Most testing tools stop measuring at the form submission, but a meaningful share of real conversion loss happens after that: how quickly the lead is routed, how long until a callback, what happens on the actual phone call. Optimizing the page while ignoring what happens after the form is optimizing the part that was rarely losing the most.

## How it is measured

The number that matters is the conversion once intake is complete - a booked appointment or a qualified handoff, not a form submission. A form fill is a candidate conversion; it is confirmed or rejected based on what happens on the call or the follow-up after it, matched through the same [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution) used across every other channel.

What this cannot claim credit for: results attributable to a change in traffic quality or volume from the channels sending people to the page in the first place. If [paid search](/services/paid-search-ppc) sends a different audience one month, page conversion rate moves regardless of any test running, and that shift is separated from the test's own result rather than letting a testing win claim credit for a traffic change.

## What we need from you

Access to whatever analytics and session recording tool is already in place, or agreement to add one where none exists. Call recordings or a transcript sample from the intake team, since the post-form review depends on hearing what actually happens on those calls. And a realistic traffic number per page before committing to a testing timeline, so the plan is built against real volume rather than assumed volume.

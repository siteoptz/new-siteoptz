This page does not have a performance number to report, and it would be dishonest to invent one. Production does not decide what gets tested and does not determine whether a test wins or loses - it turns a validated hypothesis into the specific files a platform can actually run. Its job is delivery: the right assets, in the right specs, on the schedule the testing plan requires, and its failure mode is not a disappointing number. It is a testing calendar that stalls because a hypothesis is approved and nothing is ready to run against it.

That makes this the one page on the site where "how did it perform" is answered by looking at the test itself, since production has no performance number of its own to check against. The value here sits upstream of performance entirely: how fast a validated idea becomes something real, and how consistently that keeps happening month after month without the testing plan waiting on it.

## How it is measured

There is no conversion metric for this page, and none gets manufactured to fill the space. Its conversion definition is delivery itself: the agreed volume, in the agreed specs, on the agreed turnaround, matched against the testing plan it exists to serve - the plan whose own results still run through the same [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution) as every other program on this site. A month where every committed asset shipped on time against the plan is a successful month for this program, independent of how any individual test it fed into performed.

What this channel cannot claim credit for, or be blamed for: a test's win or loss, which belongs to the hypothesis and the audience it ran against. The one exception is a technical production failure - the wrong aspect ratio, a missing caption file, a broken link - which gets flagged and owned directly as a production miss, tracked separately from that test's reported result.

## What we need from you

Access to your current brand guidelines and existing raw material - past photography, footage, logo files - so the audit of what can be reused happens before anything new gets scheduled. A committed approval turnaround on your side that matches the one we commit to on ours, since a fast production team sitting on an unapproved concept produces the same stalled calendar as a slow one. And a standing point of contact who can make a brand-guideline call quickly when a new format raises a question the existing guidelines do not answer.

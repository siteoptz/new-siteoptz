Top of funnel is every program aimed at someone who has not yet registered that a business exists, or has not consciously connected a name to a need. Nine programs run here - [paid search](/services/paid-search-ppc), [paid social](/services/paid-social-advertising), [SEO](/services/seo), [generative engine optimization](/services/generative-engine-optimization), [answer engine optimization](/services/answer-engine-optimization), [programmatic advertising](/services/programmatic-advertising), [creative ads strategy](/services/creative-ads-strategy), [campaign launch strategy](/services/campaign-launch-strategy), and [influencer marketing](/services/influencer-marketing) - covering the full range of platforms and formats a first exposure can happen on.

## What makes measurement hard here

The defining problem at this stage is displacement, not absence. A touch at this stage almost always has a real effect, but the evidence of that effect routinely shows up somewhere other than inside the touch's own report. Someone sees a paid social ad, a programmatic placement, an influencer's post, or a search result, remembers the name, and searches for it later - often through branded search, which gets credited entirely to that later, cheaper channel. A view-through window, a rank position, or an impression count is a real signal, but it answers whether someone was exposed to something, a different question entirely from whether that exposure caused a customer, and every program on this page has some version of that gap.

The fix that recurs across almost all nine programs is the same shape even where the mechanics differ: a matched-market or geographic holdout that compares a market where the program ran against a comparable market where it did not, paired with branded-search-lift tracking that looks specifically for the delayed, displaced signal a click-based report would otherwise miss. Neither tool is specific to one channel here - they are the answer to what top-of-funnel measurement actually requires.

This is also why a report built purely from platform dashboards tends to flatter whichever program happens to sit closest to the eventual click, and quietly undercount everything upstream of it. A campaign that introduced a business to a hundred people who searched the brand a week later looks, in a click-based report, exactly like a campaign that reached no one at all. Correcting for that is a load-bearing part of the reporting - without it, budget drifts toward the channel nearest the finish line and away from whatever is actually filling the funnel.

## How these programs combine

Paid search and SEO typically split by how much organic visibility a business already has for its non-brand terms; where SEO is still building, paid search covers the gap and hands off as rankings improve. Programmatic supplies the volume display, CTV, and audio buying needs at scale once a concept is validated, while creative ads strategy is what validates the concept in the first place - the two rarely run independently of each other. GEO, AEO, and SEO together cover the full surface a search engine or an AI assistant can show a business on, each with its own mechanism but a shared reliance on the same well-structured content. Influencer marketing sits alongside paid social as a way to borrow an audience someone else already built, and campaign launch strategy runs underneath any of these specifically during the ninety days a new location or service line has no baseline yet to measure against.

## Where to start

An operator with an established brand and some existing organic visibility usually starts with SEO or the GEO/AEO pair, depending on whether the more pressing gap is conventional search or AI assistant visibility, since both compound in value the longer they run and are the cheapest programs on this page over time. An operator opening a new location or entering a market for the first time starts with campaign launch strategy specifically, since every other program on this page assumes a baseline already exists to measure against, and that one is built for the ninety days before it does.

An operator with paid budget already committed but no real read on what it produces starts with whichever paid program is receiving the largest share of that budget today, run through a holdout before any reallocation decision gets made. Chasing a bigger number without first confirming the current one is real is the most common way top-of-funnel budget gets spent twice: once on the program, and again correcting for a decision made on a number that was never trustworthy.

Every program here ultimately reports through the same [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution) that connects it to a booked outcome, and the programs that convert this stage's demand into someone who has decided but not yet purchased are covered at [middle of funnel](/services/middle-of-funnel).

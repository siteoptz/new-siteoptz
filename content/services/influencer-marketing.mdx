Most influencer marketing agency relationships still run on a media kit's two numbers, impressions and follower count, and both can be purchased directly. That does not make either one useless for judging a creator's actual audience quality, but it makes them unsuitable as a measurement of what a specific paid partnership produced, since a business paying for a post has no way to separate genuine reach from reach that was bought to inflate the pitch.

Tracked links and discount codes solve part of that problem and create a new one. They tell you something real about the people who clicked through and used the code, but they badly undercount a creator's actual effect, because the common response to seeing a creator recommend a business is not clicking a link at all - it is remembering the name and searching it directly later, which shows up as branded search and gets credited to that channel instead of to the creator who actually caused it.

## How it is measured

The number that matters is the branded search and booking lift a matched-market test produces, comparing markets where a creator's content ran against comparable markets where it did not over the same window. That lift figure feeds the [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution), giving a creator partnership the same standard of proof any paid channel on this site is already held to.

What this channel cannot claim credit for: raw impressions or follower count reported by a media kit, and any tracked-link click volume presented on its own without the matched-market comparison behind it. Either number in isolation describes reach a creator claims to have, separate from any effect a business can actually verify.

The test itself needs enough markets and enough time to produce a real answer, which varies with how much natural week-to-week variation the brand's branded search volume already shows in a given region. A business with only two or three comparable markets available will need a longer test window to reach a trustworthy result than one with a dozen similar-sized markets to draw a comparison group from, and the required window is calculated upfront, before a creator relationship is signed, using the same sample-size logic applied to any other incrementality test on this site.

## What we need from you

Whoever owns the brand's search and analytics access needs to make branded search volume available broken out by market, since the matched-market comparison depends on seeing that data at a regional level rather than as one national number. Legal or marketing sign-off on usage rights terms before the first creator contract goes out, so every subsequent agreement follows the same template. And enough lead time before a launch date to run creator vetting properly and size the matched-market test window correctly, since a rushed vetting process is exactly how a bot-inflated account gets signed.

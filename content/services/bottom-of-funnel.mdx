Bottom of funnel, the final stage of the marketing funnel, is every program working on demand that already exists - someone who already found the business, already interacted with it, or already has some relationship to it on the way to becoming a paying customer. Seven programs run here - [conversion rate optimization](/services/conversion-rate-optimization), [marketing operations](/services/marketing-operations), [organic capture](/services/organic-capture), [email marketing](/services/email-marketing), [retargeting](/services/retargeting), [creative production](/services/creative-production), and [partnerships](/services/partnerships) - none of them trying to reach someone new, and none of them splitting credit across a long, undecided consideration window.

## What makes measurement hard here

Every program on this page starts with an advantage no top-of-funnel or middle-of-funnel program has: a real, warm population already in hand. That advantage is also the measurement trap. Marketing channels pointed at people who already visited the site, already opened an email, or already know the business by name will produce bookings almost no matter what they do, because that population was already closer to converting than a cold one before the program ever touched them. The honest question at this stage is never whether a booking happened after the program ran - a booking was frequently going to happen regardless. It is whether that specific booking would still have happened without the program at all, which is an incrementality question, not a volume one.

That question only gets answered by deliberately withholding a program from a comparable slice of the same warm population and watching what changes. It is a different failure mode from earlier stages: top-of-funnel measurement worries about a touch's evidence surfacing somewhere else, later; middle-of-funnel measurement worries about splitting credit fairly across several touches; bottom-of-funnel measurement worries about a channel claiming credit for a conversion it never actually caused, simply because it was aimed at someone who was already going to convert.

Consider a retargeting campaign shown only to people who abandoned a shopping cart or visited a pricing page twice. Almost everyone in that audience was already close to a purchase decision, so the campaign's reported return will look excellent even in the version where the ads changed nothing at all - a purely mechanical consequence of who the audience already was. A branded search ad, an email to an existing customer list, and a partnership commission on a referred sale all share that identical structural risk, dressed in different mechanics. Two of the seven programs on this page, marketing operations and creative production, sidestep the distortion entirely: their conversion definition is the other five programs' measurability and supply, a job that never touches a customer directly.

## How these programs combine

Marketing operations is the plumbing every other program on this page depends on to be measured accurately at all - a lead-routing or deduplication problem there quietly corrupts the numbers retargeting, email, and organic capture all report. Retargeting ads and email both re-engage the same already-interested population through different channels, and frequency between the two needs active coordination so the same person is not chased by both at once. Organic capture and conversion rate optimization both work on demand that has already reached the business's own systems - a profile view or a landing page visit - and hand off to each other at the point a lead becomes a real conversation. Creative production supplies the volume of fresh assets that retargeting in particular consumes to avoid audience fatigue, and partnerships depend on the same reconciliation discipline marketing operations already applies to every other lead source.

## Where to start

An operator who has never run a holdout on any bottom-of-funnel program starts with whichever one currently reports the best-looking return, since that is precisely the program most likely to be coasting on demand it never actually created. An operator whose leads are getting lost, duplicated, or misrouted starts with marketing operations instead, since nothing else on this page can be measured accurately until that gets fixed first. And an operator running retargeting and email at the same time, with no coordination between them, starts by simply comparing the two campaign calendars side by side - a surprising number of accounts discover they have been sending the same offer through both channels on the same day for months.

Programs here report through the same [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution) as every other stage, and the demand converted here originated in the programs covered at [top of funnel](/services/top-of-funnel).

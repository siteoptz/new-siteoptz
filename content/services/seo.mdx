You cannot turn organic search off for two weeks to see what happens without it — the holdout test that works cleanly for a paid channel does not exist here. That is the first thing that makes organic measurement different: incrementality has to be inferred from ranking movement and traffic patterns rather than established with a controlled experiment. The second problem is scale. A single national rank number is close to meaningless for an operator with a dozen locations, since a query like "urgent care near me" returns a different result in every zip code it is searched from.

We measure it the way it actually behaves: a scan grid per location, tracking visibility across the radius each site actually competes in, not one number pretending to represent all of them.

## How it is measured

Because organic cannot be held out for a controlled test, incrementality is measured indirectly: ranking position change on the specific queries being targeted, correlated against non-brand organic traffic and, eventually, against booked outcomes matched through the [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution). A ranking improvement that never shows up in booked outcomes gets reported as exactly that — a ranking improvement, not a result.

What this channel cannot claim credit for: traffic and rankings on branded or local queries, which belong to organic capture, or visibility inside an AI assistant's generated answer, which is a different mechanism measured separately as generative engine optimization. Non-brand SEO is judged on non-brand queries only.

## What we need from you

Administrative access to your website's CMS, or a direct line to whoever maintains it, since technical fixes typically require code or template changes that cannot happen through an analytics dashboard. Read access to Google Search Console and analytics for the domain. And patience calibrated correctly: technical and on-page fixes can move metrics within weeks, but authority-driven ranking improvements build over quarters, and we will tell you which category a given recommendation falls into rather than let both sound equally fast.

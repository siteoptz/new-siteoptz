Meta will report more conversions from a campaign than actually happened, and the mechanism is not fraud - it is view-through attribution. The platform counts a conversion any time someone shown an ad later converts within its attribution window, whether or not that person ever consciously noticed the ad, clicked it, or was influenced by it at all. An impression scrolled past in half a second gets treated the same as a deliberate click. That is why the number in Ads Manager is reliably higher than the number in a CRM, and why comparing the two directly makes paid social look worse than it is, or better than it is, depending on which side of the gap gets quoted.

We resolve that by testing prospecting's actual effect directly - geographic holdouts and matched-market tests that show what happens to bookings in markets where a campaign runs against markets where it does not.

## How it is measured

The number reported is lift, measured through a holdout or matched-market comparison and expressed as bookings attributable to a campaign running versus not running, not the platform's own reported conversion count. That lift figure is what feeds into the [attribution layer](/services/marketing-attribution), reported on the same terms as every other channel's number.

What this channel cannot claim credit for: a conversion from someone who had already decided to buy and would have converted through another channel regardless of the ad. Prospecting campaigns are measured on incremental lift specifically to avoid that overlap; a platform's raw conversion count makes no attempt to separate the two.

## What we need from you

A steady supply of creative - at minimum a handful of new concepts monthly, since audience fatigue sets in faster on prospecting campaigns than most teams expect. Administrative access to the Meta ads, LinkedIn, and TikTok accounts across whichever platforms are in use. And agreement on which markets can be held back for testing purposes, since a genuine holdout requires leaving real demand unaddressed in a comparison market for the length of the test.
